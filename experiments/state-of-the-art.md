# State of the art on the five benchmarks

Collected before starting GEPA optimisation, so there is a target to aim at and
a record of what the target was when we aimed at it. Every figure below is from
a primary source and the source is named. Where a number could not be verified
from a primary source it says so rather than carrying a plausible value.

## The headline: these are not one number each

Almost every figure in this literature is **pair classification**: the model is
handed candidate pairs generated by someone else's blocking step and labels each
one match or non-match, on a fixed train/valid/test split. SERF does the whole
task — blocking, matching, merging, closure — from the raw tables. The two are
different problems and the difference favours whoever was handed their
candidates, because blocking recall is the ceiling on end-to-end recall and
none of the pair-classification numbers pay for it.

A second, subtler difference: the leaderboards score the official test split of
candidate pairs, while SERF scores a 1,000-record sample of the full tables at
seed 42. Those are different denominators, not just different tasks. Neither
set of numbers bounds the other.

So the table below is a target and a sanity check, not a scoreboard SERF is on.

## Where SERF currently sits

End-to-end resolution, 1,000-record sample at seed 42, three iterations,
`gpt-oss-120b`, per-dataset signatures, **no training**, partition contract.
Printed next to the best verified pair-classification figure purely as a
sanity check on magnitude — see the warning at the top before reading anything
into the gap.

| Dataset        | SERF end-to-end | Best pair classification | Note                                            |
| -------------- | --------------: | -----------------------: | ----------------------------------------------- |
| DBLP-ACM       |           99.26 |                    99.32 | both effectively saturated                      |
| DBLP-Scholar   |           96.65 |                    95.64 | SERF's sample is 1k of 66,879 records           |
| Walmart-Amazon |           94.67 |                    90.54 | SERF's sample is 1k of 24,628 records           |
| Abt-Buy        |           93.18 |                    96.40 | SERF's sample is 1k of 2,173, so nearly the set |
| Amazon-Google  |           83.90 |                    85.21 | the hardest of the five for everyone            |

The two datasets where SERF reads highest, DBLP-Scholar and Walmart-Amazon, are
also the two where its sample is the smallest fraction of the full table, which
is exactly where a sampled score is most likely to be optimistic: sampling by
match group removes most of the non-matching records that a full run has to
reject. Abt-Buy is the closest to a full-table run and reads 3.2 F1 below the
best published figure. That ordering is a reason to be suspicious of the first
two numbers, not a reason to celebrate them.

## What this says about the GEPA target

- **DBLP-ACM is done.** 99.26 against a 99.32 ceiling, and the dataset's own
  literature calls it the easiest of the five. There is nothing here for GEPA
  to win, and a gain on it would most likely be noise.
- **Amazon-Google is the one to optimise.** It is the hardest for everyone
  (85.21 is the best anyone reports), SERF is 1.3 F1 below that, and it is
  where the contract change cost the most (-0.0265). It has the most headroom
  and the clearest signal.
- **Abt-Buy has the largest verified gap**, 3.2 F1, and is the dataset whose
  sample is closest to its full table, so a gain there is the most likely to be
  real.
- **DBLP-Scholar and Walmart-Amazon need a full-table or holdout number before
  optimisation**, because their current figures are the least trustworthy of the
  five and optimising against an optimistic number optimises against the
  sampling.

## Caveats

- **Different splits under the same dataset name.** ComEM (arXiv 2405.16884)
  reports Walmart-Amazon 88.56 and Abt-Buy 88.24, but on its own candidate
  generation: Ditto scores 77.34 on its Abt-Buy against 89.79 on the standard
  split. Those numbers are internally consistent and not comparable to this
  table, so they are excluded from it.
- **Structured, dirty and textual variants.** DBLP-ACM, DBLP-Scholar and
  Walmart-Amazon each exist in a structured and a dirty variant, and Abt-Buy is
  textual. Figures here are the structured or textual variant as the source
  reports it; the dirty variants score lower and are not mixed in.
- **DeepMatcher's own numbers are the floor, not the target**: Abt-Buy 62.80,
  Amazon-Google 69.30, Walmart-Amazon 66.90. They are quoted in SERF's prompt
  docstrings as context for how hard each task is, not as competition.
- **Unverified, deliberately excluded.** The OpenSanctions paper (arXiv
  2603.11051) reports DeepSeek-14B at 98.23 and GPT-5.2 Pro at 98.75, but on the
  OpenSanctions Pairs dataset, not on any of these five. It is easy to misread
  those as figures for this table and they are not.
- Papers With Code was sunset in 2025; its entity resolution boards are
  mirrored at [OpenCodePapers](https://opencodepapers-b7572d.gitlab.io/benchmarks/entity-resolution-on-abt-buy.html).
  Only the Abt-Buy and Amazon-Google boards resolve there; the other three
  datasets' boards 404, so their figures here come from papers directly.
